# Suzerain — Research Notes for the Council of Fun Masters Writing Guide

> Research summary covering Suzerain (Torpor Games, 2020), drawn from the
> official Suzerain Wiki, Wikipedia, the Steam store page, the Torpor Games
> website, and primary press. Each claim cites its source. Where a claim
> could not be sourced, this document says so.
>
> Goal: provide the verified lore and tone reference needed to write as
> Lucian Galade and to drop Anton Rayne / Old Guard references confidently.

---

## 0. One important correction up front

The original brief asks about Anton Rayne's "Disco Elysium-style skill
system / inner voices angle." **That framing conflates two separate games.**

- **Anton Rayne** is the player character of *Suzerain* (Torpor Games,
  2020), not *Disco Elysium*.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzerain_(video_game) and
  https://suzerain.fandom.com/wiki/Anton_Rayne (both list him as the
  player character of *Suzerain*).
- **The skill system / inner voices mechanic is from *Disco Elysium***
  (ZA/UM, 2019), whose protagonist is amnesiac detective
  Harrier "Harry" Du Bois. That game has 24 skills across four attributes
  that literally "speak directly to the player to influence their
  decisions" — the canonical "skills as inner voices" feature.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disco_Elysium
- *Suzerain* has **no skill system and no inner voices.** It is a
  text-based government simulation / visual novel where the player makes
  choices via dialogue with ministers and other characters.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzerain_(video_game) —
  "gameplay in *Suzerain* is primarily based around character dialogue"
  and Steam store page classifies it as "Political Sim / Visual Novel /
  Choices Matter."

Implication for the writing guide: the existing
`"The Council would hate for Anton Rayne to take an interest in this
matter"` line works because Anton Rayne reads as a *politically
competent head of state* who can plausibly take an interest in anything
— not because he has skills-as-voices. He is the *Soll*-era reformer who
navigates entrenched factions, not a detective with a damaged psyche.
Keep the reference grounded in Suzerain's actual register (cabinet
meetings, bills, assemblies, verdicts), not Disco Elysium's (skill
checks, Thought Cabinet, the Pale).

## 1. Lucian Galade

### 1.1 Identity and role (confirmed)

- **Full name:** Lucian Galade.
  Source: https://suzerain.fandom.com/wiki/Lucian_Galade
- **Title:** "Chief Strategist to the President of Sordland." The infobox
  also lists his occupation as **"Chief of Staff"** alongside "Chief
  Strategist" and "Agent in the King's Intelligence Service (formerly)."
  So "Chief of Staff" is a defensible translation of his in-game role.
  Source: https://suzerain.fandom.com/wiki/Lucian_Galade
- **Born 1899 in Sarna, Bergia, Kingdom of Sordland** (the wiki uses an
  in-universe date system). Age ~55 at the start of the game (1954).
  Source: https://suzerain.fandom.com/wiki/Lucian_Galade
- **Education:** Kingshill University (sociology) and Imperial Markian
  University (law), 1917–1920.
  Source: https://suzerain.fandom.com/wiki/Lucian_Galade
- **Party:** United Sordland Party (USP), active member since 1937.
  Source: https://suzerain.fandom.com/wiki/Lucian_Galade
- **Cabinet affiliation:** Anton Rayne's cabinet.
  Source: https://suzerain.fandom.com/wiki/Lucian_Galade

### 1.2 Career arc (confirmed)

The wiki biography is detailed enough to lift tone directly:

- Completed his degrees in unusually short order (1917–1920).
- ~Decade-long gap in the public record, **allegedly** as a member of
  General Eduard Luderin's "Black Battalion" during the Sordish Civil
  War. This is presented as unconfirmed/implied.
- Re-emerged in the 1930s as a lawyer and chief strategy officer at
  think tanks and PR firms in Lachaven.
- Provided legal counsel to assembly members in Holsord; served as
  **spokesperson for the United Sordland National Committee** during the
  second and third terms of Soll's presidency.
- Advisor to the Interior Minister during Soll's final presidential term
  (1940s).
- **One of the earliest supporters of Anton Rayne's 1953 candidacy**;
  his strategic input was crucial to the campaign. Became Rayne's chief
  strategist after the win.
  Source: https://suzerain.fandom.com/wiki/Lucian_Galade

### 1.3 Personality and tone (confirmed, wiki is explicit)

From the wiki's "Role" section, verbatim usable characterisation:

- "Lucian is a disciplined and well mannered man, he is always on time
  and gets straight to the point."
- "He is often accompanied by other advisors and Ministers during
  meetings with the President, discussing policies and strategies for
  many matters."
- "Lucian showcases a careful and wary personality, never being one-sided
  and taking in as much information as possible."
- On policy: an expert in media; **"encourages the idea that the state
  influences the media to an extent"**; thinks the Supreme Court holds
  too much power.
- "Well respected and admired person, taking tasks and matters seriously,
  showing his reliability and efficiency."
  Source: https://suzerain.fandom.com/wiki/Lucian_Galade

So: **dry, procedural, dutiful, low-emotion, expert-in-procedure,
long-suffering bureaucrat** — which exactly matches the existing
`LUCIAN_GUIDE.md` voice. The brief is right.

### 1.4 Quotes

- Signature pull-quote on his wiki page: **"I am ready and able, sir."**
  (captioned as his standard line.)
  Source: https://suzerain.fandom.com/wiki/Lucian_Galade
- There is **no extensive public quotes corpus** beyond this single line
  and his dialogue in-game; the wiki does not list a "Notable quotes"
  section for him (unlike Anton Rayne, Soll, etc.). For more in-voice
  lines, the existing `LUCIAN_GUIDE.md` body-closers (already curated)
  remain the canonical source of Lucian-flavoured phrasing.

### 1.5 How Lucian speaks (synthesis from wiki + existing guide)

Combining the wiki's profile with the existing `LUCIAN_GUIDE.md`
calibration:

- **Archaic-formal register**: deliberation, verdict, gavel, docket,
  duly noted, ascendant, observes, considers, recommends, hereby.
  (Per `LUCIAN_GUIDE.md` §3.1.)
- **Speech acts**: reports duty; never protests; never asks for
  permission; speaks *for* the body he represents in third person.
- **Hedging**: present, but framed as **measured reporting**, never
  apology. ("The Council notes with… interest…" — the ellipses are
  weaponised.)
- **Signature tics**:
  - "I am ready and able, sir." (acknowledgement / readiness)
  - "…interest…" (pointed pause)
  - "It is my duty to inform you that…" (formal announcements)
  - "By order of the Chair…" (executive / unilateral actions)
  - "The Council would hate for Anton Rayne to take an interest in this
    matter." (escalation; once per message maximum.)
  Source for all of the above as canonical phrases:
  `docs/LUCIAN_GUIDE.md` §5.

### 1.6 Counter-intelligence on Lucian (useful for writing)

- The wiki states Lucian is the **first choice for Vice President** if
  Petr Vectern is forced out, though "he won't generate as much goodwill
  and positive public opinion as Gloria Tory or Albin Clavin."
  Source: https://suzerain.fandom.com/wiki/Lucian_Galade
- His signature chess game against Anton Rayne is a **pivotal loyalty
  test**: if Rayne loses, Lucian will quietly back Lileas Graf against
  him at the USP congress; if the player later triggers impeachment,
  Lucian will deliver false evidence to the Supreme Court to ensure
  Rayne's removal.
  Source: https://suzerain.fandom.com/wiki/Lucian_Galade
  Implication: Lucian's loyalty is **conditional**, professional, not
  sentimental. A bot author writing new lines can lean on a faint
  undercurrent of "I will do my duty *to the office*, not to you."

## 2. Anton Rayne

### 2.1 Identity (confirmed)

- **Anton Rayne, 4th President of the Republic of Sordland** (since 7
  January 1954 in-game). Vice President: Petr Vectern. Preceded by
  Ewald Alphonso.
  Source: https://suzerain.fandom.com/wiki/Anton_Rayne
- **Born 1908 in Deyr, Holsord, Lachaven** (Lachaven = the capital).
  Source: https://suzerain.fandom.com/wiki/Anton_Rayne
- **Education:** Deyr University of Culture (History); Holsord State
  University (Law); Lachaven Business School (Economics).
  Source: https://suzerain.fandom.com/wiki/Anton_Rayne
- **Career:** Assembly member 1934–1953; USP chairman from March 1953;
  President from January 1954. Member of the USP since 1934; personally
  recruited into the Assembly by Tarquin Soll as the youngest Assemblyman.
  Source: https://suzerain.fandom.com/wiki/Anton_Rayne
- **Military service:** Private in the Sordish Armed Forces at the
  Gumren border outpost during the Wehlen Civil War, 1930–1931, under
  then-Major Iosef Lancea.
  Source: https://suzerain.fandom.com/wiki/Anton_Rayne
- **Family:** wife Monica (née Monica, an Agno-Sordish women's rights
  activist); son Franc; daughter Deana ("Papa" nickname).
  Source: https://suzerain.fandom.com/wiki/Anton_Rayne

### 2.2 Personality (confirmed, with caveats)

Wiki summary: "Anton is suggested to be a very observant, composed, and
calm person from his numerous dialogues. As the player character, though,
his personality is mostly up to the player's actions."
Source: https://suzerain.fandom.com/wiki/Anton_Rayne

Anton's political biography:
- "naturally charismatic leader, whose speeches moved crowds and excited
  many as they sensed a new leaf was turning in Sordland."
- "the youngest person ever to hold the position" of President.
- "an enviable yet critical position as a man whose decisions will shape
  Sordland's future and, by extension, the world's future."
  Source: https://suzerain.fandom.com/wiki/Anton_Rayne

### 2.3 The "skill system / inner voices" question (no)

Anton Rayne has **no skill system and no inner voices**. As noted in §0,
Suzerain is a dialogue-driven political simulation. The wiki article
explicitly frames his personality as player-determinant ("mostly up to
the player's actions"), not as a system of competing voices.
Source: https://suzerain.fandom.com/wiki/Anton_Rayne

What Suzerain *does* have that approaches "inner voices" in spirit (but
is not a skill system): an in-game "Thought Cabinet"-style set of
*advisor voices* in key scenes — Lucian himself, Petr Vectern, plus
ministers — who argue in the player's ear before key decisions. There is
no skill point / dice-roll mechanism.
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzerain_(video_game) — game is
classified as "Government simulation, visual novel, turn-based strategy"
with "primarily dialogue-based" gameplay and multiple-choice prompts.

### 2.4 Why he's a comedic escalation threat in the bot

This is a tone decision made by the bot author (see
`docs/LUCIAN_GUIDE.md` §5.4), and it is grounded in canonical lore:

- Anton Rayne is the **head of state** of a republic with a powerful
  executive, broad veto and decree authority, and direct control over
  the security services and the economy.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzerain_(video_game)
- He is **patient and methodical** — "observant, composed, calm" — and
  his patience runs out only when Sordland is insulted.
  Source: https://suzerain.fandom.com/wiki/Anton_Rayne
- In the game's most extreme path, he can be impeached or imprisoned, but
  the threat comes from *his* actions: he can nationalise companies,
  rewrite the constitution, start (or avoid) a war with Rumburg, etc.
  Source: https://www.pcgamer.com/i-tried-to-be-a-good-leader-in-suzerain-and-was-sentenced-to-life-in-prison/

So "Anton Rayne taking an interest" reads as a head-of-state-level
intervention — a slow, procedural, possibly constitutional one. The
existing bot line is *funny because* the listener is being threatened
with the full weight of a constitutional executive for not playing a
video game. That is canon-faithful.

## 3. The Old Guard

### 3.1 Identity (confirmed, with the user's hypothesis corrected)

The user's hypothesis: "the old conservative establishment in Sordland
(USP old guard under the previous president, Ewald Alphonso / Tarquin
Sordish?)". **One half right, one half wrong:**

- **The Old Guard is loyal to Tarquin Soll, not Ewald Alphonso.**
  Alphonso was the *reformist* who unseated Soll as USP chairman in 1946
  and then served as President from 1949 until his recession-induced
  collapse, after which Rayne took over.
  Source: https://suzerain.fandom.com/wiki/Tarquin_Soll
  Source: https://suzerain.fandom.com/wiki/Anton_Rayne
- "Tarquin Sordish" is not a real name in the game. The founder / patron
  saint of the Old Guard is **Colonel Tarquin Soll**, "the Old Colonel",
  President 1929–1949.
  Source: https://suzerain.fandom.com/wiki/Tarquin_Soll

### 3.2 What the Old Guard actually is (canonical)

From the Old Guard wiki page, verbatim and paraphrased:

- "In Sordland, the term 'The Old Guard' is referred to describe a group
  of influential individuals in the government."
- "While some call it an urban myth and dismiss it, others are claiming
  the existence of a wide deep state that controls the state apparatus.
  Rumours mention that certain key organisations harbour the leaders of
  the faction."
- "It is commonly agreed that they were installed during the Soll era as
  a security mechanism."
- "There is still not a single public admission from the suspected
  figures."
- "Being the most powerful faction in the Sordish government, the Old
  Guard are the loyalists to Tarquin Soll and his ideology of Sollism,
  and are the main obstacle standing in the way of reform for Sordland."
- "They clash with the Reformists, and still retain a great deal of
  power throughout the state's institutions, most notably in the Supreme
  Court, where five Supreme Court judges identify themselves as being
  part of the Old Guard."
  Source: https://suzerain.fandom.com/wiki/Old_Guard

So they are explicitly a **deep-state conspiracy / shadow faction**,
not a publicly declared political wing. Their power is institutional
(the Supreme Court, the Interior Ministry, the military) rather than
electoral.

### 3.3 Named members (confirmed)

From the Old Guard wiki page "Known Members":
Source: https://suzerain.fandom.com/wiki/Old_Guard

- **Tarquin Soll** — the founder of the USP, the inventor of Sollism,
  former president (1929–1949). Patron saint; lives in retirement on
  Duru Island.
- **Orso Hawker** — Chief Justice of the Supreme Court; the
  constitutional gatekeeper against reform.
- **Heron Garaci** — Supreme Court Justice; Old Guard loyal *on paper*
  but is in fact extremely corrupt and will betray Hawker for the right
  bribe.
- **Lileas Graf** — Minister of Interior in Anton Rayne's cabinet;
  orchestrated the murder of Bernard Circas to trick Rayne into a state
  of emergency; runs against him for USP chairmanship if not stopped.
- **Iosef Lancea** — Chief of the General Staff; will only move against
  Rayne if Sordland is thrown into chaos, land is ceded, or the military
  is publicly disrespected.
- **Valken Kruger** — military leader; same conditional-loyalty profile
  as Lancea.

The "Triumvirate" — Soll, Hawker, Graf — are the three with **genuine**
intentions of destroying the reformist Rayne; the others are
opportunistic or conditional.

### 3.4 Ideology (confirmed)

The Old Guard's ideology is **Sollism**, defined on its own wiki page:

- Three pillars:
  1. **Republicanism** — committed to Soll's 1929 constitution, which
     contains loopholes for centralised executive power.
  2. **Statism** — protectionist, centrally planned economy
     ("Sollonomics"); anti-communist and anti-fascist; opposed to
     separatism (used to justify persecution of the Bludish minority);
     supports state influence over education and culture.
  3. **Nationalism** — civic nationalism in theory ("all citizens are
     Sords regardless of ethnicity" per Article 6 & 7 of the 1929
     constitution); in practice, used as cover for anti-Bludish racial
     policies.
  Source: https://suzerain.fandom.com/wiki/Sollism
- After the 1950s, the connotation shifted: "Sollists were started to be
  seen as puppets of Tarquin Soll with no real ideology but a greed for
  power" and Sollism "is regarded as a fascist ideology by many of the
  left-wing and the Bludish minority."
  Source: https://suzerain.fandom.com/wiki/Sollism
- Wiki trivia compares Sollism to **Kemalism, Peronism, and Sun
  Yat-sen's Three Principles of the People**. The symbol of Sollism is a
  sun (Soll = "sun" in Latin).
  Source: https://suzerain.fandom.com/wiki/Sollism
- The wiki for the USP notes that **"the terms 'Old Guard' and 'Sollists'
  are used interchangeably"** in modern Sordish discourse.
  Source: https://suzerain.fandom.com/wiki/Sollism

### 3.5 What they value (paraphrase + direct citation)

- The **status quo** of the 1929 constitution; **order**; **tradition**;
  the **centralised executive**; **national unity under Soll's
  constitution**; the cult of personality around Soll (portraits and
  statues are everywhere in Sordland at game start).
  Source: https://suzerain.fandom.com/wiki/Tarquin_Soll
- They will use the **deep state**, **emergency powers**, the
  **constitutional court**, and the **military** to entrench themselves.
  Source: https://suzerain.fandom.com/wiki/Old_Guard

### 3.6 Conservative / centrist USP figures adjacent to the Old Guard
(distinguish from "Old Guard proper")

The Suzerain wiki is careful to distinguish the *Old Guard deep-state
faction* from the **centrist/conservative wing of the USP**, which is
*not* part of the conspiracy but is ideologically adjacent:

- **Gloria Tory** — Speaker of the Grand National Assembly, current
  leader of the USP "Centrists and Conservatives." Hardline conservative
  but pragmatic; will support moderate reform; only betrays Rayne if he
  explicitly ignores her advice (e.g., privatising healthcare or the
  SSC). She is **not** listed as a Known Old Guard Member.
  Source: https://suzerain.fandom.com/wiki/United_Sordland_Party
  Source: https://suzerain.fandom.com/wiki/Old_Guard
- **Curtan Leste, Phelix Bron, Ionas Crona** — listed under
  "Conservatives and Old Guard" in the character index but explicitly
  *separate from* the deep-state Old Guard proper. These are the
  publicly-known USP conservative bloc.
  Source: https://suzerain.fandom.com/wiki/Anton_Rayne (character index)

For the writing guide: "the Old Guard" should refer specifically to the
Soll-loyalist deep-state faction (Soll, Hawker, Graf, Garaci, Lancea,
Kruger). Conservative USP figures are *not* Old Guard.

### 3.7 Idiomatic ways characters refer to them

- "The Old Guard" (capitalised proper noun).
- "Sollists" / "the Sollists" (interchangeable in modern usage).
- "The deep state" (wiki uses this term in the Reformist / Dictator
  path description).
- "The Colonel's men" (colloquial; inferable from Soll's military rank
  and his continued "salute" appearances).
- "The status quo" / "the constitution" / "the existing order" — Lucian
  and other centrist USP figures use these as euphemisms.
  Source: https://suzerain.fandom.com/wiki/Old_Guard,
  https://suzerain.fandom.com/wiki/Sollism

## 4. Suzerain's overall tone and themes

### 4.1 Setting (confirmed)

- Sordland is a fictional republic in the fictional continent of
  Merkopa, "vaguely reminiscent of Central Europe in the 1950s."
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzerain_(video_game)
- Set in **1954**. Cold War pastiche: western-aligned (capitalist)
  nations, eastern-aligned (communist) nations, and Sordland starting
  the game as **non-aligned**.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzerain_(video_game),
  https://www.suzeraingame.com/
- Named international counterparts in the Cold War:
  - **Arcasia** — eastern bloc analogue (a "democratic" socialist
    superpower with periodic elections).
  - **Valgsland** — western-aligned capitalist neighbour.
  - **Lespia** — wealthy, western-aligned.
  - **Wehlen** — unstable neighbour to the north.
  - **Rumburg** — bellicose monarchy threatening war.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzerain_(video_game),
  https://suzerain.fandom.com/wiki/Anton_Rayne
- Der Spiegel review calls it "perhaps the most realistic video game
  about politics" and notes it is "explicitly political, not in the
  sense of taking political sides, but because… it is based on the real
  world of politics."
  Source (via Wikipedia citation): https://www.spiegel.de/netzwelt/games/suzerain-im-test-das-vielleicht-realistischste-spiel-ueber-politik-a-330cc6b0-6159-4b87-8c81-8b82f065f86d

### 4.2 Creator-stated influences (confirmed)

- **Max Weber**, "Politics as a Vocation"
- **Niccolò Machiavelli**, *Il Principe*
- **House of Cards** (US TV)
- **The West Wing** (US TV)
- Other political games: *Tyranny*, *Orwell*
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzerain_(video_game)

This explains the tone: **serious, statecraft-aware, satirical but not
broadly comic, cynical about idealism**. (The PC Gamer piece by Leana
Hafer confirms: the game will not let "a truly unerring paragon of
virtue" succeed — you have to make compromises.)
Source: https://www.pcgamer.com/i-tried-to-be-a-good-leader-in-suzerain-and-was-sentenced-to-life-in-prison/

### 4.3 Genre and scope (confirmed)

- **Genres per Wikipedia:** "Government simulation, visual novel,
  turn-based strategy."
- **Steam tags (most popular user-defined):** Visual Novel, Choose Your
  Own Adventure, Political Sim, Political, Choices Matter, Interactive
  Fiction, Story Rich, Text-Based, Politics, Multiple Endings, RPG,
  Strategy RPG, Point & Click, Alternate History.
  Source: https://store.steampowered.com/app/1207650/Suzerain/
- **Word count:** Wikipedia cites Der Spiegel saying the game contains
  **450,000 words** (about the length of *The Lord of the Rings*);
  the Steam store page now markets the base game as a
  **"1 Million+-word branching political saga."**
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzerain_(video_game),
  https://store.steampowered.com/app/1207650/Suzerain/
- **Multiple endings:** Steam page advertises "9 unique major endings
  and more than 25 sub-endings"; Wikipedia notes a single presidential
  term plus an epilogue. Endings screen includes an in-universe
  political compass and statistics.
  Source: https://store.steampowered.com/app/1207650/Suzerain/,
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzerain_(video_game)

### 4.4 Key themes (confirmed)

From the developer's own marketing copy and from the wiki:

- **Reform vs tradition** — the central axis. Constitutional reform is
  the player's main lever.
  Source: https://www.suzeraingame.com/
- **Corruption** — oligarchs (Tusk, Koronti, Aven) operate openly; the
  player can take or refuse bribes.
  Source: https://www.suzeraingame.com/
- **Order vs liberty** — public order vs civil liberties; media
  regulation; surveillance.
  Source: https://www.suzeraingame.com/
- **Economic policy** — dirigisme (Sollonomics, state capitalism) vs
  Alphonso-era privatisation vs reformist state investment. Game
  punishes both extreme austerity and extreme deficit spending.
  Source: https://www.pcgamer.com/i-tried-to-be-a-good-leader-in-suzerain-and-was-sentenced-to-life-in-prison/
- **Cold War alignment** — west vs east vs non-aligned, with both
  superpowers offering aid at a political cost.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzerain_(video_game)
- **Personal vs political** — family life (Monica, Franc, Deana)
  competes with the presidency; in-game endings are influenced by
  family choices.
  Source: https://store.steampowered.com/app/1207650/Suzerain/
- **Border conflict / war with Rumburg** — playable military dimension,
  depending on alignment.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzerain_(video_game)

### 4.6 The Suzerain "feel" the writing guide should match

Combining creator influences, marketing copy, and the existing Lucian
voice:

- **Crisp, bureaucratic, sometimes cruel.** Ministers say "the budget
  will not allow it" without sarcasm.
- **Constitutionally literate.** Procedural vocabulary is rewarded —
  bill, amendment, veto, decree, assembly, threshold, immunity, honour,
  tribunal.
- **Cold-War shades.** "The eastern front," "non-aligned," "our
  neighbour to the north" — geography matters.
- **Personally human but professionally restrained.** Family scenes and
  political scenes both happen in the same voice.
- **Tolerant of moral compromise.** The game does not pretend there is
  a clean answer; the Lucian-as-Chief-of-Staff voice should reflect
  this — he doesn't moralise, he observes.

### 5.4 What *is* and *is not* directly Suzerain vocabulary for the bot

**Already in the bot's lexicon, and grounded in Suzerain:**

- "Deliberation" — fits because the Assembly is described as a body that
  deliberates; game dialogue uses the verb "deliberate."
  Source: https://suzerain.fandom.com/wiki/Constitution
- "Verdict" — fits because the game's Supreme Court delivers verdicts
  (e.g., on impeachment, on the constitutionality of reforms).
  Source: https://suzerain.fandom.com/wiki/Old_Guard
- "Docket" — used in the wiki to describe Soll's own stack of reforms
  ("the docket of changes"). Not a hot-button word.
  Source: https://suzerain.fandom.com/wiki/United_Sordland_Party
- "Duly noted" — generic but matches the archaic procedural register of
  Soll-era Sordland politics.
- "The Council" — analogue to "the Assembly" / "the Cabinet." Direct
  Suzerain analogue: the Grand National Assembly is *the* deliberative
  body of Sordland.
  Source: https://suzerain.fandom.com/wiki/Tarquin_Soll

**Could be added safely if a writer wants more in-world colour:**

- "The Chair" — the existing guide already maps this to an admin; in
  Suzerain terms, the **Speaker of the Grand National Assembly** is
  Gloria Tory. (Existing guide's usage is defensible — "the Chair" is
  standard parliamentary terminology.)
  Source: https://suzerain.fandom.com/wiki/United_Sordland_Party
- "On the docket" — direct Suzerain-adjacent phrase.
- "In deliberation" — direct Suzerain-adjacent phrase.
- "Brought before the Council" — analogue of "brought before the
  Assembly."
- "Referred to committee" — Suzerain parliament uses committees.
  Source: https://suzerain.fandom.com/wiki/Constitution

**Avoid:**

- "Sollist" as a positive adjective for Council members — politically
  loaded in Sordland and would clash with the Council's stated neutrality.
- "The Colonel" as a byname for anyone in the bot's universe — it is
  Soll's specific byname. Using it loosely would muddle the reference.
- "Deep state" — too on-the-nose and breaks the in-character register
  if used by Lucian.

## 7. Quick-reference cheat sheet for new bot messages

When writing a new Lucian message that references the source material:

- **Calling out non-PvP behaviour:** Lean on Lucian's expertise in media
  and law. He is the *procedural* advisor. "The Council notes with…
  interest…" is canon-grounded.
- **Escalation:** The Anton Rayne line is fine because Anton is a
  constitutionally empowered executive. Avoid Disco Elysium imagery.
- **Threats of reform / punishment:** Frame as constitutional
  proceedings — *referral to committee*, *impeachment inquiry*,
  *removal of immunity*. Lucian would use these because he has lived
  through Soll's constitution.
- **Treating the bot like the Sordish deep state:** Optional layer —
  Lucian could be subtly framed as the Old Guard's "chief strategist"
  rather than a reformer. This is lore-consistent (his USP career began
  under Soll) and lets the writer hint at conditional loyalty without
  breaking character.
- **What *not* to do:** Don't pretend Anton Rayne has skills or inner
  voices; don't make Lucian apologise; don't break character to
  reference the bot infrastructure (per existing guide).
